# agy-staff 0.8.0 — Bench v1 findings in the lead, jobs and worker instructions

## Changes since 0.7.4

[agy-staff Bench v1](../../bench/README.md) ran 0.7.3 on Claude Code and Codex over SWE-bench Verified, SWE-Atlas QnA and DeepSearchQA. Its trajectories showed where delegation lost time, tokens or correctness. This release moves those findings into the instructions. The companion runtime and the job lifecycle are unchanged.

**Waiting.** Every wake-up rereads the host's whole context, so the jobs skill now asks the host to wake as rarely as it can. When the host is known to wake the model on a background command's completion (such as Claude Code), the host ends its turn after launching the wait instead of sleeping or polling. Otherwise it stays in its turn and sets the longest supported block explicitly (5–10 minutes, or the tool's maximum such as 300,000 ms in Codex `write_stdin` / `// @exec`, or a foreground `wait <id> --timeout 10m` in synchronous-shell hosts like Pi and OpenCode). In v1, Codex made 872 collection calls over 20 trials, most returning after the default of about 30 seconds; the skill now names the Codex parameters (`write_stdin` `yield_time_ms: 300000`, or the code-mode `// @exec` pragma).

**Briefs.** The host copies the user's constraints and prohibitions into the brief verbatim, in their own section, and keeps orchestration instructions such as "use `$agy:lead`" out of it. In v1, paraphrased briefs dropped a benchmark rule 7 times in 20, and forwarded orchestration instructions led one worker to launch a nested agy job in the same worktree. Briefs now separate settled decisions from open ones after the host checks the facts they rest on, and a deadline sets the job's `--timeout` and appears in the brief.

**Delegation and acceptance.** Lead still delegates substantive work by default and now states the trade-off: delegation saves the host's context and quota, not total time or tokens. A tightly coupled change stays in one assignment with its investigation and verification. Acceptance reads the worker's status and reported conflicts first, checks interface choices, departures from the brief and figures reported as verified, and stays well below the cost of redoing the work. The host fixes small, understood defects directly and sends larger gaps back with `continue`.

**Workers.** The staffer, research and implement templates share a short set of working rules:

- do the assignment without delegating further;
- follow the brief's scope and decisions, and report confirmed contradictions between the brief's factual premises and what the worker observed under `## Conflicts with the task` instead of building on a false premise;
- respect a stated deadline and prefer a partial report with named gaps;
- write the final message only after all work has finished, beginning with `Status: DONE`, `DONE_WITH_CONCERNS` or `BLOCKED`.

The review template gains the delegation, deadline and final-message rules. The research template drops its defensive framing, asks for full source documents once a search finds them, never repeats a query, and stops searching after two similar results; in v1, timed-out research jobs spent up to 93 searches on snippets. The implement and research templates ask for whole reads of small files, searches with context lines, an exploration stop once the change can be named, and reusable probe scripts; in v1 the worker took a median of 104.5 steps per SWE-bench job, one tool call per step.

`docs/DESIGN.md` records the shared worker rules, the brief boundary for orchestration instructions, the cost of each wake-up, and the revised lead defaults. Package, Claude Code, and Codex manifests are versioned at 0.8.0.

  - **Host waiting and cost:** explicit 300-second blocking waits (`yield_time_ms: 300000` on both `// @exec` and `write_stdin` / `functions.wait`) cut collection calls across the 20 trials from **842 to 273 (−67.6%)**, host turns from **1,118 to 543 (−51.4%)**, and mean host cache reads from **1.89M to 1.00M tokens (−47.3%)**. Mean Codex host spend fell **32.1%** ($0.4833 → $0.3283), turning v1's +25.4% host-cost premium over Codex solo ($0.3854) into a **14.8% host-cost saving vs solo** (and **8.7% below Codex native** at $0.3595).
  - **Accuracy and conflict reporting:** overall solve rate rose from **14/20 to 15/20**. DeepSearchQA reached **6/6 (100%)**, with every task scoring `f1 = 1.0, fully_correct = 1.0` across all 3 runs on both Gemini 3.8 Flash and DeepSeek V4 Pro judges (recovering `deepsearchqa-0118`, which timed out in v1). On SWE-Atlas QnA `baa04` (`kitty`), the worker opened with `Status: DONE_WITH_CONCERNS` and used `## Conflicts with the task` to separate the prompt's conflated direct-launch, `--hold`, and OSC 133 shell-integration paths.
  - **Step counts vs wall clock:** total AGY planner steps across the 20 tasks fell **8.4%** (2,309 → 2,114; **−13.6%** on SWE-bench Verified from 1,145 → 989) and raw median wall time fell on all three suites. The v2 batch ran against the `GEMINI_API_KEY` endpoint (`modelProvider: gemini`), which averaged **8.46s/step** across 2,088 measurable generation intervals versus **7.70s/step** on v1's OAuth route (71 steps exceeded 30s vs 55 in v1); individual tasks that hit clusters of >30s API responses (such as `sympy__sympy-14248`, where planner steps fell 31% from 269 to 185 but 13 steps exceeded 30s) therefore show longer raw wall times despite fewer steps. All reported times retain raw unadjusted `wall_clock_s`.
  - **Worker tool-loop ablations (why templates stay tool-agnostic):**
    1. *Single-tool loop:* across 6,690 tool-calling steps in 46 AGY transcripts, AGY executes strictly one tool call per turn (`0 / 6,690` multi-tool turns). Prompting for "parallel tool calls in the same turn" has no effect on AGY's planner loop.
    2. *Persona and effort (`staffer` medium vs `research` high):* routing `ba9a8` to `research` (`gemini-3.8-flash-high`, `s61x-v080b-r1`) reduced steps from 126 to 100 and worker spend from $1.15 to $0.87, but `high` effort increased per-step latency from **6.34s/step to 11.41s/step (1.8×)**, raising AGY runtime from 799s to 1,141s. Keeping `lead` defaulted to `staffer` (`medium`) preserves the faster per-step cadence.
    3. *Forcing `run_command` over `view_file` (`s61x-bashonly-r1`, 6 tasks):* instructing the worker to avoid `view_file` and batch reads in `run_command` eliminated all 234 `view_file` calls (`234 → 0`) and halved steps and cost on read-heavy tasks (`baa04`: 110 → 56 steps, total cost $1.50 → $0.74, raw wall time 1,556s → 756s). Across all 6 QnA tasks, however, total steps dropped modestly (585 → 565 in `v0.8.0` → 498 in `bashonly`) and mean total cost was unchanged ($1.3183 vs $1.3329). Inspection of step outputs showed why: AGY caps `run_command` stdout at **~8 KB (~150 lines, head-truncated with `<truncated N lines>`)**, whereas `view_file` returns up to **~48 KB (~700 lines)** in one call; banning `view_file` forced multi-step `sed -n` paging on 1,000-line files (`baa15`) and front-loaded tool output into context before long runtime-debugging loops (`ba9a8`, `ba9dd`). In keeping with thin personas, 0.8.0 leaves tool choice to AGY's post-trained heuristics rather than hardcoding tool-specific rules.

## Upgrading

Update the plugin and restart the host to load the revised skills and templates; Claude Code and Codex keep versioned plugin copies. See the [upgrade instructions](../REFERENCE.md#upgrading). Pi and OpenCode installations pinned to a Git ref can select `v0.8.0`.
